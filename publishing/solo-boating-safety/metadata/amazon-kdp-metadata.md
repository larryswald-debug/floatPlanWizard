## Long description

Solo boating can be peaceful, focused, and deeply rewarding. It also removes the safety margin that another capable person aboard may provide.

**Solo Boating Safety** helps recreational boaters deliberately replace some of that missing redundancy with preparation, conservative decisions, accessible equipment, practiced self-recovery, useful communications, and a responsible shore contact.

Inside the guide:

- why boating alone changes the safety equation;
- how to create a useful float plan and work with a shore contact;
- staying aboard, reboarding, and preparing for separation from the craft;
- VHF, DSC, cellphones, beacons, and communication redundancy;
- weather, cold water, fatigue, and go/modify/postpone decisions;
- readiness considerations for paddlecraft, small powerboats, sailboats, and cruisers;
- tracking limitations and safer route changes;
- a complete 84-item Solo Boater Pre-Departure Checklist; and
- a five-minute final departure routine.

The guidance is practical and non-alarmist. It explains where equipment, legal requirements, recovery methods, and operating limits depend on the craft, activity, waters, conditions, and jurisdiction.
