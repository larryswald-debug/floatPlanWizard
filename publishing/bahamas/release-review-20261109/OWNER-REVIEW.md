## Product and source assessment

The example is useful for planning decisions and arithmetic, but it is not a ready-to-navigate crossing. A member imports a private editable itinerary, chooses the actual water path using current charts and marina/authority information, supplies each leg’s measured/calculated mileage and method, confirms explicit vessel performance and reviews each leg. They can save an incomplete draft at any point. The guide’s six-step explanation names the actual planner controls. Missing mileage remains the principal product friction.

Brown’s replaces Big Game Club as the candidate destination. No additional offshore point is needed merely to fill the destination-reference model. Approach geometry, berth availability, fuel supply and actual mileage remain independent research tasks. The historical 47.6 nm comparison and illustrative 60 nm worksheet are not imported.

Current Customs page, recent-updates PDF and office directory were retrieved; conflicting legacy/manual text is disclosed. Fee tables and historical fee explanations are omitted. Detailed CBP ROAM/Florida pages remained inaccessible; the guide uses limited primary-source reporting information and directs predeparture questions to CBP. It makes no eligibility or app-clearance promise. [Source decisions](/Users/lawrencewald/Docker/cf-mysql-dev/wwwroot/fpw/publishing/bahamas/release-review-20261109/content-source-asset-review.json) preserve these limits.

Photographs are Port Everglades PIO’s 2017 staging view and Pietro’s 2014 Alice Town waterfront. Original Commons rights/revisions were checked; both are CC BY-SA 4.0 and used as historical context. Natural Earth geometry is public domain; attributed geographic labels are separate from navigational endpoints. File-specific credits, hashes and changes are in [assets.json](/Users/lawrencewald/Docker/cf-mysql-dev/wwwroot/fpw/publishing/bahamas/assets.json).
